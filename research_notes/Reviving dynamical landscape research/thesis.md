## Mathematical contributions of the thesis

### Takeaway
A central published contribution is differentiable parameter inference against bifurcation diagrams; the thesis also incorporates collaborative pattern-formation work. It presents synthetic vector-field constructions, derivative-operator spectral calculations and critical-slowing-down analysis. Their presentation in the thesis does not establish that all are original discoveries, unpublished elsewhere, or valid without their stated hypotheses; no separate priority or proof audit was conducted.

### Cited Findings
- **Bifurcation inference method (Ch 4 / NeurIPS 2021).** Gradient-based inference of ODE parameters θ producing a user-specified bifurcation diagram. "The cost function contains an error term that is minimal when the model bifurcations match the specified targets and a bifurcation measure which has gradients that push optimisers towards bifurcating parameter regimes. The gradients can be computed without the need to differentiate through the operations of the solver that was used to compute the diagram." Diagrams are computed by **deflated continuation**; gradients use implicit-layer techniques and the **generalised Leibniz rule** applied to implicitly defined steady states; the **determinant of the state-space Jacobian** is the bifurcation indicator. Demonstrated on minimal models — saddle-node `F(u,p) = p + θ₁u + θ₂u³`, pitchfork `F(u,p) = θ₁ + pu + θ₂u³` — and the genetic toggle switch; the cost landscape "allows us to organise models in terms of topological and geometric equivalence." — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/2c6ae45a3e88aee548c0714fad7f8269-Abstract.html), [chapters/4.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/4.tex)
- **Bifurcation diagrams as tangent fields.** The method views bifurcation diagrams as curves formed by intersecting implicit surfaces `f_θ(z)=0`, `g_θ(z)=0` tangent to a field `tangent(z)` and perpendicular to the gradients — i.e., bifurcation points are where the Jacobian determinant vanishes along the steady-state manifold (Appendix listing "Bifurcation Diagrams as Tangent Fields", `fig:implicit-surfaces`, `fig:determinant-field`). — [Appendices.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/Appendices.tex)
- **Hopf versus cycle scope.** Appendix E sketches an equilibrium eigenvalue measure. The repository now uses real eigenvalues' parts in its measure but retains a determinant condition in matching sensitivities; see `inference.md`. Neither damped oscillations nor a Hopf candidate establish a fitted, stable periodic orbit. Cycle continuation requires a periodic boundary-value/shooting formulation and phase fixing — [paper Appendix E](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#A5), [Objectives.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Objectives.jl), [BifurcationKit shooting](https://docs.sciml.ai/BifurcationKit/stable/periodicOrbitShooting/).
- **Unexpected scientific by-product.** Applying the method to the double-exclusive model "surprisingly… discovered a parameter regime that gives rise to a phenotype with damped oscillatory behaviour, which was previously unknown" (Ch 4 Afterword; the paper reports the same for Figure D.1). — [chapters/4.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/4.tex)
- **Basis functions for state space (Ch 2).** The thesis presents distance-based synthetic fields and a tangential construction for illustrative limit sets. Do not summarize these by imposing `F=0` along a nontrivial periodic orbit: that would make every point an equilibrium. Smoothness of distance fields, nonzero tangential flow and preservation of a desired invariant set under superposition need separate checking before using this as a guaranteed cycle-design method — [chapters/2.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/2.tex).
- **Eigenvalue distributions of derivative operators (Ch 2 §"Eigenvalue Distributions of Derivative Operators" + Appendix A).** Via the Circular Diagonalization Theorem, the K-site second-difference matrix on a ring has arcsine-law eigenvalues; the L-dimensional Laplacian (Kronecker sum) has eigenvalue density equal to the L-fold convolution of arcsine laws, with Van Hove singularities at λ=−4,0 in 1D and an elliptic-integral singularity in 2D, smoothing toward a Gaussian (a restatement of the CLT); diffusion coefficients rescale as `α(λ) → α(λ/D)/D`. The linearised reaction–diffusion operator `L = J|_{u*} + D ∂²/∂x²` then has spectra that are "distorted and transformed arcsine distributions", and a Turing instability is a spectral mass crossing zero (`fig:reaction-diffusion-spectrum`, `fig:turing-bifurcation`). — [chapters/2.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/2.tex), [Appendices.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/Appendices.tex)
- **Critical slowing down as a Bernoulli ODE (Ch 2 §"Critical Slowing Down", §"Fluctuations").** Near a field degeneracy (vanishing Jacobian eigenvalue), the dynamics along the degenerate direction obey a Bernoulli differential equation whose solution exhibits two timescales — exponential and polynomial `(t−t')^{1/(1−n)}` — the marker of critical slowing down; the Langevin/Fokker–Planck treatment gives a steady-state Gaussian near a fixed point with covariance `Σ⁻¹ = −(2/D) J|_{u*}`, i.e. variance diverges along the degenerate direction, linking bifurcations to phase-transition criticality. — [chapters/2.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/2.tex)
- **Limit-cycle theory, honestly framed (Ch 2 §"Limit Cycle Analysis").** Poincaré–Bendixson as the existence tool; "Limit cycles are inherently global structures in state space… it is not possible to know whether a local state [is] part of a limit cycle or not before we have seen a trajectory visit that state twice"; computation requires discretising the recurrence condition (trapezoid, collocation, shooting methods, citing Lust 2000, Kuznetsov 2004, Uecker 2018). — [chapters/2.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/2.tex)
- **Geometric cost for field matching (Ch 4 §"An Abandoned Approach").** An abandoned trajectory-based route: Gaussian-process smoothing of trajectory derivatives within the Delaunay-triangulation data hull, then matching a parametrised model F to the estimated field f̂ with the direction-only cost `L(θ|D) := exp(−(f̂·F)/(|f̂||F|))` — minimal when the fields point the same way, "focusing on the qualitative aspects… rather than the quantitative dynamics or kinetics". This cost is later reused in Ch 6 for limit-cycle design. — [chapters/4.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/4.tex)
- **Cusp/hysteresis extraction from cytometry (Ch 2 §"Phenotypes from Statistics in Homeostasis").** A protocol sketch for extracting limit points in a (p,p′) plane from priming/conditioning serial-dilution experiments: limit points as the level set `D(P||Q) = ε` of a distribution-similarity measure between differently-primed populations; in Ch 3 a simpler "population separation" hysteresis measure was used to avoid tuning ε (Fig S13). — [chapters/2.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/2.tex), [chapters/3.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/3.tex)
- **Ch 3 modelling contributions (author list in thesis).** Arc-length continuation of the bistability region, spatial simulations, bespoke boundary-velocity inference from microscopy movies, novel state-space analysis of boundary formation, and bistability analysis of parameterised models vs flow-cytometry hysteresis quantification; released as [gszep/double-exclusive-reporter](https://github.com/gszep/double-exclusive-reporter). — [chapters/3.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/3.tex)
- **Ch 5 software contribution.** `FlowAtlas.jl` (+ new `FlowWorkspace.jl` for parsing FlowJo workspaces): giga-scale SOM embedding (`GigaSOM.jl`) without downsampling, imputation enabling joint analysis of non-identical antibody panels (imputed values excluded from downstream statistics), OpenLayers/D3 interactive front-end; demonstrated immunological findings (regulatory T-helper Helios⁺ subsets with tissue-resident CD69⁺ phenotypes absent from blood; Th1 distribution supported on a simplex). — [chapters/5.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/5.tex), [gszep/FlowAtlas.jl](https://github.com/gszep/FlowAtlas.jl)

### Inferences
- Implicit differentiation is a reusable architectural idea, not a free PDE extension: regularity, spectral multiplicity, discretization error and linear-solve conditioning still require analysis.
- The basis-function construction is the thesis's only concrete mathematical object that can *prescribe* limit-cycle geometry, but it is a synthetic-field generator (used for illustrations), never connected to inference, data, or existence/uniqueness analysis. This is the strongest in-repo starting point for a "qualitative landscape" revival.
- The arcsine-convolution spectral machinery gives a reusable analytical handle on discretised PDE operators, but in the thesis it feeds only the *linear* (Turing) analysis.

### Gaps
- The NeurIPS supplementary derivations (measure properties, Leibniz rule for space curves) were not read directly (PDF extraction unavailable); their existence and topic are attested by the appendix table-of-contents in `Appendices.tex` and the paper's conclusion.
- No convergence proofs, complexity theorems, or existence results for the basis-function fields appear in the thesis; whether such fields can be decomposed into solenoidal/conservative parts consistently is unaddressed (sophisticated curl/divergence-decomposing kernels are only cited, Ch 4 §"An Abandoned Approach").
- Mathematical qualifications for synthesis: the covariance expression above belongs to the restrictive no-current/isotropic-noise derivation, not a general nonsymmetric-Jacobian covariance formula; the continuous arcsine density must be distinguished from a finite matrix's discrete spectrum and its scaling limit. These formulas were not independently rederived in this audit and should not be presented as general-purpose theorems.

## Assumptions underlying the thesis

### Takeaway
The thesis uses differential-equation models with several additional assumptions for particular derivations. Its no-current stationary-density calculation is a restricted route for interpreting snapshot statistics, not a blanket assumption on every deterministic vector field used in the thesis. It must not be used to claim that all oscillatory phenomena were excluded: the notes themselves report damped oscillations and synthetic-cycle illustrations.

### Cited Findings
- **Core modelling assumption.** "For the purposes of this thesis we will assume that the behaviour of the organism under study can be cast in terms of a system of differential equations" `du/dt = F(u,p)`, `F: R^{N+P}→R^N`, θ∈R^M (Ch 2 §"Differential Equation Models", eq. of the general model class); the right-hand side "in principle… can be arbitrarily complicated, containing spatial derivatives or even machine learning models", but the inference method operates on ODE fixed points. — [chapters/2.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/2.tex)
- **Biochemical assumptions.** Reaction-rate equations arise from a chemical master equation via "mean field approximation", with "two major assumptions… a thermodynamic limit where the number of molecules is sufficiently large such that correlations due to discreteness can be ignored and a sufficiently *well mixed* cytoplasm so that we do not have to model space" (Ch 2, lac-operon derivation). — [chapters/2.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/2.tex)
- **Stochastic assumptions.** Noise modelled as zero-mean white Gaussian with equal variance D in all N dimensions (Langevin), converted by Kramers–Moyal expansion to a Fokker–Planck continuity equation (Ch 2 §"Fluctuations"). — [chapters/2.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/2.tex)
- **Steady-state statistics assume no currents.** The closed-form steady-state distribution `P_θ(u,p) ∝ exp((2/D)∫F·du)` is derived "in the scenario where at steady state… there are also no circulating probability currents J_θ = 0" — i.e. a gradient-like, non-oscillatory field; near a stable fixed point this gives the inverse-Jacobian Gaussian covariance. — [chapters/2.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/2.tex)
- **Homeostasis assumption for data.** Statistical phenotype inference assumes samples are drawn from steady-state distributions (Ch 2 §"Phenotypes from Statistics in Homeostasis"); "if the data was collected at [a] time where the organism is in homeostasis, then we can infer the steady states… and any possible bifurcations directly from statistical measures". — [chapters/2.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/2.tex)
- **Linearisation assumptions.** Turing analysis proceeds by Taylor-expanding the reaction term around the homogeneous steady state and studying the linear operator `L = J|_{u*} + D∂²/∂x²` and the dispersion relation `|J|_{u*} − |k|²D − ωI| = 0` (Ch 2 §"Spatially Extended Systems", §"Turing Bifurcations"). — [chapters/2.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/2.tex)
- **Optimisation assumptions.** Gradient-based, local optimisation; distributions of optima estimated by repeated re-runs (Ch 6 §"Limitations"); positivity of parameters handled by log-transforms rather than priors. — [chapters/6.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/6.tex)
- **Basis-function assumptions.** Cross-product angular-momentum terms defined only for N=3 (wedge products needed beyond); limit sets must be connected surfaces for the tangential/cyclic construction (Ch 2 §"Basis Functions for State Space"). — [chapters/2.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/2.tex)
- **Flow-cytometry data assumptions.** arcsinh transforms, compensation matrices, hierarchical manual gating as ground-truth-ish annotations; imputation used *only* to compute embeddings, "neither visualised nor included in any downstream analysis" to avoid biasing biology (Ch 5 §"Impute & Reduce"). — [chapters/5.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/5.tex)

### Inferences
- Snapshot densities do not in general identify probability currents or distinguish static heterogeneity from motion around an orbit. Interpret the thesis's homeostasis warning within its observation/model assumptions, not as a universal impossibility theorem for all statistical inference. A cycle-inference proposal should state what temporal, perturbational or mechanistic information resolves this ambiguity.

### Gaps
- No sensitivity of the Fokker-Planck steady-state form to state-dependent or anisotropic diffusion is discussed; D is assumed isotropic.
- The thesis does not state identifiability conditions for inferring θ from bifurcation targets (only empirical cluster structure).

## Limitations acknowledged by the thesis

### Takeaway
The thesis is unusually explicit that its method is **co-dimension-one, fixed-point-only, ODE-only, and local**: demonstrated bifurcations are saddle-node and pitchfork; Hopf is an unimplemented extension; the Jacobian-determinant bottleneck confines the implementation to ODEs; limit cycles are structurally invisible to its homeostasis-statistics data route; and all gradient optimisation inherits initialisation-dependence and ill-conditioning.

### Cited Findings
- **Method scope.** The publication "focuses on parameter synthesis for a subset of bifurcations" — pitchfork and saddle-node — with "steps towards Hopf bifurcations" only (Ch 4 §"Problem Statement & Context"); the paper's conclusion: "The computational bottleneck is the evaluation of the state-space Jacobian determinant which limits the implementation to ordinary differential equations." — [chapters/4.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/4.tex), [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/2c6ae45a3e88aee548c0714fad7f8269-Abstract.html)
- **Limit cycles invisible in steady-state data.** "Unfortunately it would not be possible to distinguish whether a population is in a limit cycle or merely static, distributed across the states that make up the limit set. Therefore it is not possible to detect Hopf bifurcations from statistics in homeostasis." (Ch 2 §"Phenotypes from Statistics in Homeostasis"). — [chapters/2.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/2.tex)
- **Global-bifurcation limitation.** The thesis warns that equilibrium-local analysis does not determine global orbit geometry. Its categorical statement about no normal forms should not be repeated as a general theorem: periodic-orbit return-map normal forms and global-bifurcation asymptotics are distinct from equilibrium normal forms. This audit did not inventory those theories — [thesis discussion](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/2.tex), [BifurcationKit periodic-orbit analysis](https://docs.sciml.ai/BifurcationKit/stable/periodicOrbitShooting/).
- **Optimisation limitations (Ch 6 §"Limitations").** (i) local gradient methods are initialisation/preconditioning-dependent and "suffer from the same gradient instabilities with respect to ill-conditioned systems as other implicit layer approaches"; (ii) estimating parameter *distributions* by re-running optimisation is "computationally expensive" with "no guarantee that all regions of the distribution have been properly sampled"; global/interval-arithmetic methods are even more expensive and only applied to very low-dimensional problems; (iii) "no natural way of encoding prior knowledge in the parameters", motivating a Bayesian extension. — [chapters/6.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/6.tex)
- **Abandoned trajectory approach.** The GP field-inference route was dropped because trajectories "need… sufficient temporal resolution and sampled from a wide variety of initial conditions. Such data is not widely available" and GP inversion scales as K′³ (Ch 4 §"An Abandoned Approach"). — [chapters/4.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/4.tex)
- **Biological practicalities.** "In vivo gene regulatory networks mostly consist of unknown and experimentally inaccessible parameters", and "usually there exist multiple equally valid models" (Ch 4 preface); producing actual Turing instabilities in the engineered colony "remains challenging" (Ch 3 preface); universal function approximators "tend not to reveal the mechanism… and hence cannot be used to deduce which genetic design interventions can be made" (Ch 2 §"Phenotypes from Machine Learning Models"). — [chapters/4.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/4.tex), [chapters/3.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/3.tex), [chapters/2.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/2.tex)
- **Scaling limits for PDE design.** For discretised PDEs "the gradient calculation — which would scale like (NK)² where N is the number of states and K≫1 the number of spatial discretisations — would be the limiting factor", requiring sparse tri-diagonal methods and eigensolvers restricted to the vicinity of the imaginary axis; Lyapunov-exponent (chaos) gradients "prohibitively expensive" (Ch 6 §"Spatially Extended Systems"). — [chapters/6.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/6.tex)
- **FlowAtlas limitations.** Gating-strategy sensitivity disagreements between experts; EmbedSOM "was not designed with interaction in mind"; clustering lacks flexibility for domain-expert tail-population annotations (Ch 5 §"Discussion"). — [chapters/5.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/5.tex)

### Inferences
- The proposed revival extends the demonstrated inference workflow. Distinguish partial ingredients and illustrations from a validated end-to-end implementation rather than describing all proposed remedies as wholly unexecuted.

### Gaps
- The thesis never quantifies how far the Hopf measure extension is from working (no experiments, no cost-landscape plots for oscillatory regimes in the main text; only a supplementary figure attested).
- No discussion of stochastic limit cycles / coherent oscillations under noise (the Langevin treatment stays at fixed points).

## Future directions stated by the thesis — limit cycles, PDEs beyond linear Turing, and landscape mapping

### Takeaway
Ch 6 §"Future Work" proposes exactly the two directions the current revival names, at the level of **design blueprints without algorithms**: (1) *designing limit cycles* by combining global basis-function constraints (∂Ω, ω) with local eigenvalue constraints (Hopf measure); (2) *designing spatially extended systems* — snaking/spots/stripes via discretisation, and Turing bifurcations via differentiable dispersion relations using the implicit-function theorem — while explicitly flagging that everything beyond linear stability was then owned by others (mass-conserving systems, Halatek 2018). A third, quieter future direction is *qualitative model-space navigation*: embeddings that align "clusters of equivalent bifurcations" across models of different complexity — the thesis's own prototype of a "qualitative landscape map".

### Cited Findings
- **Designing limit cycles (Ch 6 §"Designing Limit Cycles", `fig:design-limit-cycles`).** "Designing limit cycles requires two global constraints: a closed surface ∂Ω specified in the state space u and an angular momentum field ω(u)… Basis function methods naturally give rise to an unstable focus that pushes trajectories towards the limit cycle from within." Combining the geometric cost with the bifurcation measure "can simultaneously design the shape of the limit cycle as well as the bifurcations around it"; worked example: a cycle born in a local Hopf bifurcation and destroyed in a global infinite-period bifurcation. "The key takeaway is that by combining local constraints via Jacobian eigenvalues and global constraints via basis functions in state space, it is possible to design global bifurcations." Evaluation of the model only near the limit set "can be done sparsely so [as to] save compute time." — [chapters/6.tex](https://github.com/gszep/thesis/blob/master/chapters/6.tex)
