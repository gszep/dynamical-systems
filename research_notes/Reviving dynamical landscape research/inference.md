# BifurcationInference.jl — Parameter Inference with Bifurcation Diagrams (Szep, Dalchau, Csikász-Nagy, NeurIPS 2021)

Review provenance (2026-10-19): one selective source-audit round; paper equations, current `Objectives.jl`/`Utils.jl`, thesis dating and periodic-orbit requirements were spot-checked. Original citations and researcher coverage statements are retained; the reviewer did not rerun the implementation. See `audit.md`.

## What is the paper, its authors, year, venue, and mathematical scope?

### Takeaway
The linked NeurIPS 2021 paper is **"Parameter Inference with Bifurcation Diagrams"** by Gregory Szep, Neil Dalchau, and Attila Csikász-Nagy. It demonstrates equilibrium-bifurcation parameter inference with scalar control p. Appendix E sketches an equilibrium eigenvalue measure for Hopf detection; this is not demonstrated inference of a periodic-orbit branch. Hopf points and limit cycles are different mathematical objects — [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3).

### Cited Findings
- Bibliographic record: submitted 8 Jun 2021 (v1), revised 27 Oct 2021 (v3); journal reference "Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 34 (2021)"; subjects cs.LG, math.DS, q-bio.QM — [arXiv:2106.04243 abstract page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04243).
- NeurIPS 2021 proceedings entry (paper hash `2c6ae45a3e88aee548c0714fad7f8269`) — [proceedings.neurips.cc](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/2c6ae45a3e88aee548c0714fad7f8269-Abstract.html); [camera-ready PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/2c6ae45a3e88aee548c0714fad7f8269-Paper.pdf); [NeurIPS 2021 poster page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2021/poster/26728); ACM DL entry 10.5555/3540261.3540690 ([dl.acm.org](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/3540261.3540690)); pages 5620–5630 per [KCL profile](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/people/grisha-szep).
- The repository `bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl` explicitly links and implements this paper: "This library implements the method described in Szep, G. Dalchau, N. and Csikasz-Nagy, A. 2021. Parameter Inference with Bifurcation Diagrams" using `BifurcationKit.jl` + `ForwardDiff.jl` — [README](https://github.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl#readme-ov-file).
- Stated scope restriction: "we will consider the simplest class of bifurcations known as co-dimension one bifurcations not including limit cycles" — [§1.1 Preliminaries](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S1.SS1).
- Prediction set definition (Eq 2): `𝒫(θ) := {p | ∃u: F_θ(u,p)=0, |∂F_θ/∂u|=0, d/ds |∂F_θ/∂u| ≠ 0}` — steady state + zero determinant of the state-space Jacobian + finite-slope crossing (non-degeneracy) — [§1.1, Eq (2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S1.SS1).
- Demonstrated models: minimal saddle-node `p+θ₁u+θ₂u³` and pitchfork `θ₁+pu+θ₂u³` (N=1, M=2); genetic toggle switch (N=2, M=5, Hill n=2, Eq 11); double exclusive reporter synthetic gene circuit in E. coli (N=4, M=21, Supp D) — [§3](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S3).
- Beyond matching, the cost landscape "allows us to organise models in terms of topological and geometric equivalence" — a stated main contribution — [§1 contributions](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S1).

### Inferences
- The method is best understood as *inverse bifurcation analysis made differentiable*: qualitative observations (where behaviour changes as an experimental knob p is turned) become supervised targets `𝒟`, and the solver-computed diagram `𝒫(θ)` is matched to them by gradient descent.
- Mathematical scope is deliberately narrow (codimension-1, static, real eigenvalues, scalar p); everything about Hopf/limit cycles and PDEs lives in Supplementary E and forward-looking statements (see sections below).

### Gaps
- Semantic Scholar citation API rate-limited during this session (HTTP 429), so its citation count could not be independently confirmed; OpenAlex was used instead (see successor-work section).

## What is the objective (cost function), and how does gradient differentiation avoid differentiating through the solver?

### Takeaway
For nonempty prediction and target sets, Eq 4 averages target-wise geometric means; Eq 8 adds `(|𝒫|−|𝒟|) log Ψ`. This encourages matching but does not guarantee convergence or discovery completeness. Implicit differentiation and a moving-integral rule avoid differentiating the continuation iteration trace under the paper's regularity assumptions; they do not eliminate forward solves, local derivative calculations or linear solves — [paper §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3).

### Cited Findings
- Error term (Eq 4): `E(θ,𝒟) = (1/|𝒟|) Σ_{p′∈𝒟} Π_{p(θ)∈𝒫(θ)} |p(θ)−p′|^{1/|𝒫|}` — geometric mean over predictions avoids cross-terms and penalises unmatched targets; zero iff each target is matched, and permits |𝒫| ≥ |𝒟| — [§2.1.1, Eq (4)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S2.SS1.SSS1).
- Bifurcation measure (Eq 5): `φ_θ(s) := (1 + ||∂F_θ/∂u| / (d/ds)|∂F_θ/∂u||)^{-1}`; equals 1 at non-degenerate bifurcations, decays slowly away from them so gradients persist; per B.5–B.6 it can also approach 1 when dλ/ds → ±∞ without a crossing (spurious maxima) — [§2.1.2, Eq (5)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S2.SS1.SSS2); [Supp B, Eq (B.5)–(B.6)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#A2.SS1).
- Tangent vector: the directional derivative d/ds requires a tangent, obtained as the nullspace of the rectangular N×(N+1) Jacobian `∂F_θ/∂(u,p)·T_θ(s) = 0` (Eq 6), reliably computed by QR (nullity known) — [§2.1.2, Eq (6)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S2.SS1.SSS2).
- Total measure (Eq 7): `Ψ(θ)` = arclength-weighted average of φ over the level set F_θ(u,p)=0, normalised so Ψ→1 when the control range is densely bifurcating — [Eq (7)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S2.SS1.SSS2).
- Cost (Eq 8): `L(θ|𝒟) := (|𝒫|−|𝒟|) log Ψ(θ) + E(θ,𝒟)`; the count-dependent coefficient changes the direction of the measure contribution and removes it when counts match. The authors' annealing interpretation is not a global optimization guarantee — [§2.1.2, Eq (8)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S2.SS1.SSS2).
- Gradient (Eq 9): `∂L/∂θ` decomposes into the Ψ term plus the error term; crucially |𝒫| itself carries no gradient (piecewise-constant count) — [§2.2, Eq (9)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S2.SS2).
- Bifurcation-point gradient (Eq 10): `∂p/∂θ = −p̂·[∂G_θ/∂(u,p)]^{-1}·∂G_θ/∂θ` with `G_θ(u,p) := [F_θ(u,p); |∂F_θ/∂u|]`, solved as linear systems (not explicit inversion); the (N+1)×(N+1) Jacobian becomes singular exactly in the degenerate case d/ds|∂F/∂u| = 0 — [§2.2, Eq (10)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S2.SS2).
- Line-integral gradient: generalised Leibniz rule (Supp C) moves d/dθ inside the integral; the integration-region term needs only the **normal deformation field** `dz/dθ = −∂F/∂z^⊤ (∂F/∂z ∂F/∂z^⊤)^{-1} ∂F/∂θ` (Eq C.8, Moore–Penrose inverse of the rectangular Jacobian), valid because tangential reparametrisation is arbitrary (Eq C.9) — [Supp C, Eq (C.1)–(C.11)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#A3).
- Overall claim: "the gradient ∂L/∂θ can be evaluated using automatic differentiation methods without needing to back-propagate through the solver that obtained the level set" — [§2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S2.SS2); framework follows implicit layers (Look et al. 2020; Bai et al. DEQ 2019) — [§1, §2.2 references 22–23](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S1).

### Inferences
- The architecture avoids storing a reverse-mode tape of all continuation iterations. Total memory still includes branch states, derivatives, factorisations and solver workspaces; a blanket O(diagram size) memory bound was not established.
- Because the error-term gradient uses the "velocity" of the bifurcation point w.r.t. θ (Eq 10), the method can also place bifurcations at target *states* u′ by using the full deformation matrix instead of the p̂ projection (stated in §2.2).

### Gaps
- The paper expression needs a convention at an empty prediction set, and logarithms require a usable positive measure. Exact-match/singular cases and implementation gradients were not numerically regression-tested in this audit. Paper/code equivalence is qualified by the normalization difference below.

## How is continuation actually performed, and what does the released implementation really do?

### Takeaway
The released code uses deflated continuation but is not identical to the main-text objective: its eigenvalue/sinh measure is windowed and summed over branch arclength **without Eq 7's length normalization**; its target error uses squared distances. The gradient's augmented condition remains determinant-based. An eigenvalue measure alone does not establish a working Hopf-target or cycle-inference pipeline — [Objectives.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Objectives.jl).

### Cited Findings
- Paper: "To compute the diagram we use a predictor-corrector method called deflated continuation [24, 25] developed for partial differential equations" (Farrell, Beentjes & Birkisson 2016; Veltz, BifurcationKit.jl) — [§1](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S1).
- `deflationContinuation` in the source: seeds roots per parameter value via `findRoots` with `DeflationOperator` (deflated Newton, `maxRoots=3` default), then runs `PALC()` via `ContIterable` forwards and backwards from every root, collecting `Branch` vectors; branches kept if their midpoint lies inside the parameter range — [src/BifurcationInference.jl (master)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/BifurcationInference.jl).
- `train!` loop: each iteration recomputes the full deflated continuation, evaluates `∇loss`, updates θ via Flux optimisers (default `Momentum(0.001)`; the README example uses `ADAM(0.01)`; the paper used gradient descent η=0.01 and ADAM η=0.1), stops when Loss < 0.02 and |predictions| = |targets| — [src/Utils.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Utils.jl); [README](https://github.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl#basic-usage).
- Released `measure` (differs from main-text Eq 5): uses **real parts of eigenvalues** λ and their directional derivatives dλ along the tangent, `mapreduce((λ,dλ) -> 2/(2 + |sinh(2λ)/dλ|), +)` — i.e., a bounded per-eigenvalue form of the Supplementary-E Hopf measure (E.1), summed over eigenvalues, times an erf `window_function` over [p_min, p_max] — [src/Objectives.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Objectives.jl).
- Released `errors`: arithmetic mean over targets × geometric mean over predictions of **squared** distance `(z.p − p′)²` (paper Eq 4 is unsquared); `∇errors` correspondingly uses `2·velocity/(z.p−p′)` — [src/Objectives.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Objectives.jl); [src/Gradients.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Gradients.jl).
- `velocity` implements Eq (10) exactly: `distance(F,z,θ) = [F(z,θ); det(∂Fu)]`, adjoint solve of the augmented Jacobian with the p-direction unit vector `[0;1]`, then AD over θ — i.e., the **determinant condition is still used for locating/matching fold-type predictions** even though the measure is eigenvalue-based — [src/Objectives.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Objectives.jl).
- `∇measure` per branch point: adjoint-style solve `∂implicit ← linsolver(−∂Fz', ∇_z measure)`, then `∇_θ (measure + F·∂implicit)`, plus `measure·∇region`; `∇region` contracts `t′∇deformation t` per θ component with `deformation = −∂Fz\∂Fθ` (least-norm solution = Moore–Penrose form C.8) — [src/Gradients.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Gradients.jl).
- Tangent: `tangent_field` = QR-based nullspace (`kernel`) of the rectangular Jacobian, normalised to unit norm (Eq 6) — [src/Objectives.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Objectives.jl); [src/Structures.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Structures.jl).
- AD through eigenvalues: a custom `eigen` for `Dual` matrices (Giles 2008 matrix-derivative results) computes forward-mode derivatives of eigenvalues/eigenvectors — [src/Objectives.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Objectives.jl).
- Complexity (paper §3.3): bottleneck is the determinant/state-Jacobian evaluation, "scales like N²" (their Figure 5, Intel i7-6700HQ, no GPU); gradient method convergence ~√M in the number of parameters vs ~M² for Nelder-Mead — [§3.3, Figure 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S3.SS3).
- Supp D reports a practical fix for spurious measure maxima: apply `tanh` to the determinant to flatten gradients in non-bifurcating regimes (Eq D.1), used for the N=4, M=21 E. coli circuit — [Supp D, Eq (D.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#A4).

### Inferences
- Source-audit correction: `measure(branch)` and `measure(branches)` are weighted sums, not a normalized average. Therefore code Φ is not constrained to [0,1]; branch length and branch discovery can affect its scale. Calling this an equivalent or uniformly improved objective is unwarranted. The code's geometric-mean helper uses empty-product initialization 1 — [Objectives.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Objectives.jl), [Utils.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Utils.jl).
- Each `train!` iteration recomputes continuation and then evaluates the gradient; source inspection does not establish which dominates runtime. Proposed cost accounting: restarts × iterations × (continuation + objective/AD + linear algebra), with compilation/setup separated — [Utils.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Utils.jl).
- Forward-mode AD is not independent of the number of differentiated directions. Measure state and parameter scaling separately; the paper's observed convergence scaling is not a general complexity theorem for gradient evaluation.

### Gaps
- `Plots.jl` and the `test/` suite were not read (rendering and regression coverage not assessed here).
- The Figure 5 "N²" claim is reported as stated; no independent complexity analysis was performed (determinant/LU is classically O(N³) in general, so the paper's measured scaling plausibly reflects small-N constants or state structure of test model Eq 13).

## What are the assumptions, the degeneracy conditions, and the limitations?

### Takeaway
The method assumes: a **scalar** control condition; **static codimension-1** bifurcations with a **simple real eigenvalue crossing zero with finite slope** along a regular 1-D steady-state curve (non-null, non-degenerate intersection of the N implicit surfaces in ℝ^{N+1}); and that continuation finds the relevant branches. The **degenerate case** d/ds|∂F/∂u| = 0 (e.g., cusps, higher-order contact) is excluded by construction — it is precisely where the augmented Jacobian ∂G/∂(u,p) becomes singular and the gradient (Eq 10) ceases to exist. The cost is only **piecewise smooth** (undefined gradient on contours where the prediction count changes), and the main practical limitations are the determinant/eigenvalue evaluation bottleneck (ODEs with N≲100), no limit cycles in the main scope, and no PDE-scale support.

### Cited Findings
- Non-degeneracy condition embedded in Eq (2): the determinant must cross zero "with a finite slope... d/ds|∂F_θ/∂u| ≠ 0. This is the non-degeneracy condition" — [§1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S1.SS1).
- Supp B gives necessary & sufficient conditions (B.1): an eigenvalue λ(s) with λ(s*)=0 and dλ/ds|_{s*} ≠ 0; "for now we... restrict λ ∈ ℝ"; cases with both zero require higher-order derivatives d^nλ/ds^n and "are the cases we refer to as degenerate and are not considered here" — [Supp B, Eq (B.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#A2).
- Curve regularity assumption (Supp A): "assume that the intersection of these N surfaces exists and is not null or degenerate", giving 1-D space curves F_θ(z)=0 with a globally-defined tangent field T_θ(z) (Eq A.2) — [Supp A, Eq (A.1)–(A.7)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#A1).
- Gradient existence: "The cost function is piece-wise smooth and differentiable with undefined gradients only in parameter contours where the number of predictions |𝒫| changes; this is when Ψ(θ) is undefined and the inverse of ∂G_θ/∂(u,p) does not exist" — [§2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S2.SS2).
- Hopf is invisible to the determinant: the main-text measure "only quantifies... zero crossings in the determinant"; Hopf detection "can no longer be compactly written in terms of the determinant" and needs the eigenvalue-sum measure (E.1) — [Supp E, Eq (E.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#A5).
- Measure misbehaviour: φ can approach 1 without a bifurcation when dλ/ds → ±∞ (B.6) — the tanh fix (D.1) addresses this; the released code's sinh(2λ) form similarly bounds the far-from-bifurcation regime — [Supp B Eq (B.6)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#A2); [Supp D Eq (D.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#A4); [Objectives.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Objectives.jl).
- Stated limitation: "our implementation currently does not scale well for partial differential equations where a large [number] of states N arises from discretisation of the spatial variables. The only reason we need this determinant is because it is an indicator... One approach would be to take the product of a finite subset of eigenvalues... any more efficient calculation must still permit back-propagation through it"; "tractable on laptop computers for states N<100" — [§3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S3.SS3).
- Conclusion restates: "The computational bottleneck is the evaluation of the state-space Jacobian determinant which limits the implementation to ordinary differential equations" — [§4](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S4).
- Error term permits extra predictions at zero error, but unmatched targets can incur positive geometric-distance error even before the count term is considered — [§2.1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S2.SS1.SSS1).
- Reliance on continuation completeness: predictions are only those bifurcations found by deflated continuation; in code, root seeding uses `maxRoots=3` per seed value and requires at least one root found (asserts) — [src/BifurcationInference.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/BifurcationInference.jl).

### Inferences
- A determinant zero is not by itself a full bifurcation classification; branch regularity and non-degeneracy need checking, especially at symmetric pitchfork/transcritical intersections. For a smoothly tracked conjugate eigenpair at Hopf, one scalar `Re λ=0` condition is independent, not two duplicate real-part equations.
- Near degeneracy the augmented solve can become singular or ill-conditioned. No theorem was verified that the loss always repels cusps or guarantees smooth optimization; do not infer that from the non-degeneracy assumption.
- Piecewise smoothness means restarts matter in practice (the paper's 800 restarts for the toggle switch, 98% converging to two clusters — [§3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S3.SS2)).

### Gaps
- The paper does not discuss bifurcations of the *diagram computation itself* (e.g., continuation steps failing near sharp folds) beyond try/catch in code; robustness statistics per-iteration are not reported.
- No treatment of noise or identifiability of θ beyond the geometric/topological equivalence clustering.

## What would extension to periodic orbits and to PDEs actually require?

### Takeaway
Separate three tasks: detect a Hopf candidate on an equilibrium branch; fit its parameter location with a Hopf-specific augmented system; and infer a **periodic orbit** using shooting/collocation, unknown period, a phase condition and cycle observables/stability. An equilibrium eigenvalue crossing is not a cycle solver or a stable-cycle guarantee. PDE extension additionally needs discretization/convergence control, not merely faster dense linear algebra — [BifurcationKit shooting and Floquet documentation](https://docs.sciml.ai/BifurcationKit/stable/periodicOrbitShooting/).

### Cited Findings
- Hopf extension (paper): measure over real parts (E.1) detects "the onset of damped oscillations and emergence of limit cycles via Hopf bifurcation"; "Used together with a steady state solver that detects periodic solutions and gradient-based optimisation, we can specify regions of damped oscillation and limit cycles"; measures "can be used sequentially or in parallel to encourage optimisers to run through a sequence of bifurcations" — [Supp E, Eq (E.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#A5); [§4](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S4).
- Manifolds (paper): "Our approach generalises naturally to bifurcation manifolds such as limit point curves or surfaces... the normal components of implicit derivatives can still be calculated for under-determined systems of equations [28,36,37]. In the case of manifolds it would be more appropriate to use isosurface extraction algorithms rather than continuation" — [§4](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S4).
- PDE bottleneck (paper): determinant evaluation limits to ODEs; suggested remedy = product of a finite subset of eigenvalues, "must still permit back-propagation" — [§3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S3.SS3).
- Code-level fold hard-coding: `distance(F,z,θ) = [F(z,θ); det(∂Fu(F,z,θ))]` and `velocity` solve against this augmented system only — [src/Objectives.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Objectives.jl).
- Code-level dense linear algebra: `kernel` uses `qr(A').Q` (dense QR) or `nullspace` (SVD); `realeigvals` calls dense `eigen`; gradients use ForwardDiff over full state vectors; `getParameters` sets continuation step to the grid step with `nev=N`; a code comment flags "todo@(gszep) matrix inverse is computational bottleneck" — [src/Structures.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Structures.jl); [src/Gradients.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Gradients.jl); [src/Utils.jl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bifurcationkit/BifurcationInference.jl/master/src/Utils.jl).
- Deflated continuation was itself developed for PDEs (disconnected diagrams of PDE systems) — Farrell, Beentjes & Birkisson 2016, cited as [24] and used as the diagram engine — [§1](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S1).
- The Leibniz-rule machinery is parametrisation-agnostic (fixed domain S, arbitrary γ_θ; tangential freedom fixed by choosing normal deformations, C.9), so integrals over other implicitly-defined solution sets carry over in principle — [Supp C, Eq (C.1)–(C.11)](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#A3).

### Inferences
- **Proposed staged extension:** first test Hopf-location sensitivities using a tracked simple eigenpair and transversality; then differentiate a phase-fixed periodic-orbit system with unknown period. Damped oscillations at a stable focus are not sustained cycles. Cycle stability requires Floquet information; shooting sensitivities may use variational equations, forward AD or adjoints, not necessarily adjoints alone — [shooting, Jacobians and Floquet methods](https://docs.sciml.ai/BifurcationKit/stable/periodicOrbitShooting/).
- **PDE research gaps:** sparse/matrix-free derivatives and iterative solves address computational scaling, but do not resolve boundary conditions, spectral truncation, conditioning, mode tracking, symmetries or continuum validity. No universal infeasibility threshold at N≈10⁴ was established.
- **Two-parameter exploration:** equilibrium surfaces and bifurcation loci are different objects. Fold/Hopf loci are generically curves in two control parameters; a generic codimension-two cusp is an isolated point there, not a cusp curve. Extra defining equations are needed beyond extracting F=0.

### Gaps
- No maintained periodic-orbit or PDE extension of this specific pipeline was found anywhere (see next section); these requirements are derived from the paper+code, not from a published extension.
- No complete differentiated Hopf-target/cycle-target implementation was verified. A tracked-eigenvalue formulation and a formulation with explicit eigenvectors/frequency have different unknowns and normalization equations; these need a dedicated derivation, not two redundant conjugate real-part conditions.

## Relevance to efficient qualitative landscape exploration and nonlinear pattern detection

### Takeaway
For the stated goal — exploring qualitative behaviour landscapes and detecting nonlinear patterns — the paper's durable contributions are (i) a *loss landscape over model parameter space whose basins are topological-equivalence classes and whose minima manifolds are geometric-equivalence classes*, navigable by gradient descent from qualitative targets alone (no time-series data), and (ii) a differentiable, continuation-based structural pattern detector (eigenvalue/determinant crossings along equilibrium curves). Its practical ceiling for that goal is the per-iteration cost of a full deflated-continuation sweep and the small-N ODE restriction.

### Cited Findings
- Landscape semantics: "the optimal θ* is... in general a manifold representing the space of qualitatively equivalent models"; basins contain "the correct number and type of bifurcations... All models within this basin are in some sense topologically equivalent. This hierarchical classification allows us to identify the set of models that satisfy observed qualitative behaviour before any attempt at inferring kinetic parameters" — [§1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S1.SS1); [§3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S3.SS1).
- Empirical landscape exploration pattern: 800 random restarts → 98% converge to two qualitative clusters (mutual activation vs inhibition) for the toggle switch; 21-parameter model → four optimal regions found by sampling optima and embedding them with GigaSOM (self-organising maps) — [§3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S3.SS2); [Supp D](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#A4).
- Pattern-detection instances: in the E. coli double-exclusive-reporter model the method "found a damped oscillatory regime that was not known about in the original paper" — [§4](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S4).
- Data-philosophy: qualitative observations "should precede any attempt at inferring other properties" (citing Stumpf & Roesch 2019); trajectory-based fitting "over-constrain[s] information originating from the kinetics and dynamical transients" — [§1](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S1).
- Efficiency envelope: gradient descent converges in ~√M vs ~M² for Nelder-Mead; N<100 states tractable on a laptop CPU, no GPU — [§3.3, Figure 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04243v3#S3.SS3).

### Inferences
- The method is a *qualitative-behaviour explorer by restarts*: gradient descent supplies local navigation to a requested diagram; restarts + clustering of optima supply the global map of qualitative regimes. That matches the "landscape exploration" goal directly, but the global step is sampling-based, not amortised (no surrogate/emulator), so exploration cost scales with restarts × (continuation sweep per iteration).
- As a *nonlinear pattern detector*, its primitives (measure φ over curves, eigenvalue crossings, windowing to a parameter interval in code) generalise: any qualitative event expressible as a regular level-set crossing along an implicitly defined manifold can, in principle, be given a differentiable indicator — this is the paper's most reusable idea, independent of its specific ODE scope.
- For pattern-formation PDE landscapes (the thesis motivation), the current implementation is out of reach; the realistic route is re-deriving the same adjoint machinery on a matrix-free continuation stack (BifurcationKit supports matrix-free problems), substituting the indicator per §3.3.

### Gaps
- No benchmark numbers exist (in the paper or repo) for landscape-exploration throughput (restarts/hour) beyond the convergence plots; any efficiency claim beyond √M scaling is inference.
